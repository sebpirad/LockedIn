# Rejected quotes — "Locked in" quote research

Famous lines that were considered and **not** used, with the reason. "Not found" (where stated) means I searched the
named primary text (Project Gutenberg / Wikisource full text) and the line is not there; claims about
Quote Investigator (QI) findings are summarised from memory of QI's articles and should be re-checked by the verifier.

| Quote (as usually circulated) | Usual attribution | Why rejected |
|---|---|---|
| "Insanity is doing the same thing over and over again and expecting different results." | Albert Einstein | Misattributed. Not in Einstein's writings; QI traces early versions to 1980s recovery literature (Narcotics Anonymous text, Rita Mae Brown 1983). |
| "We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act, but a habit." | Aristotle | Will Durant's paraphrase in *The Story of Philosophy* (1926). The genuine Aristotle (NE II.1, Ross translation) is used instead as q064. |
| "It does not matter how slowly you go as long as you do not stop." | Confucius | Not in the Analects (Legge text searched). No early source found. The genuine "raising a mound" passage (Analects IX.18) is used instead as q008. |
| "Our greatest glory is not in never falling, but in rising every time we fall." | Confucius | Appears in Oliver Goldsmith's fiction (*The Citizen of the World*, 1760s), put in the mouth of a fictional Chinese philosopher; not in the Analects. Not used under either name because I could not open the Goldsmith text to confirm wording. |
| "Success is not final, failure is not fatal: it is the courage to continue that counts." | Winston Churchill | Misattributed (no source in Churchill's speeches/writings; the International Churchill Society lists it as not his). The Harrow 1941 "Never give in" is used instead (q079). |
| "If you're going through hell, keep going." | Winston Churchill | No source in Churchill's works; listed by ICS among misattributions. |
| "Never, never, never give up." | Winston Churchill | Misquotation of "never give in … except to convictions of honour and good sense" (Harrow, 29 Oct 1941). |
| "Whatever you can do, or dream you can, begin it. Boldness has genius, power and magic in it." | Goethe | Not in *Faust*; a loose 1835 paraphrase by John Anster combined with W. H. Murray (1951). QI covers this. |
| "Be the change that you wish to see in the world." | Gandhi | Not found in Gandhi's writings in this form; a paraphrase of a longer 1913 passage. Genuine Gandhi (*Young India*, 1920) used instead. |
| "First they ignore you, then they laugh at you, then they fight you, then you win." | Gandhi | No source in the Collected Works; QI traces the pattern to a 1914 trade-union speech (Nicholas Klein). |
| "The secret of getting ahead is getting started." | Mark Twain | No source in Twain; QI finds earlier unattributed versions. |
| "Twenty years from now you will be more disappointed by the things you didn't do…" | Mark Twain | Not in Twain's works; QI points to H. Jackson Brown Jr.'s *P.S. I Love You* (1990). |
| "Whether you think you can, or you think you can't — you're right." | Henry Ford | Only late, unsourced attribution; not traced to Ford in his lifetime with certainty. |
| "Nothing in the world can take the place of persistence…" | Calvin Coolidge | Not found in Coolidge's speeches/writings; QI finds it circulating anonymously earlier. |
| "Do one thing every day that scares you." | Eleanor Roosevelt | QI traces it to Mary Schmich's 1997 column. Genuine Roosevelt line from *You Learn by Living* (1960) used instead (q093, secondary). |
| "It is never too late to be what you might have been." | George Eliot | Not found in Eliot's novels, letters or poems; earliest appearances are 20th-century. Genuine Eliot (*Daniel Deronda*) used instead. |
| "If you hear the dogs, keep going…" / "Every great dream begins with a dreamer…" | Harriet Tubman | No primary source; Tubman left no writings and these lines are not in her contemporary biographies (Bradford 1869/1886). |
| "Luck is what happens when preparation meets opportunity." | Seneca | No locatable passage in Seneca; widely described (e.g. by QI-style investigations) as a modern attribution. I did not run a full-text search myself. |
| "The secret of change is to focus all of your energy, not on fighting the old, but on building the new." | Socrates | From Dan Millman's novel *Way of the Peaceful Warrior* (1980), spoken by a character named "Socrates". |
| "Patience is bitter, but its fruit is sweet." | Jean-Jacques Rousseau / Aristotle | Proverbial; no source found in Rousseau or Aristotle. |
| "Don't judge each day by the harvest you reap but by the seeds that you plant." | Robert Louis Stevenson | Not found in Stevenson's works. Genuine Stevenson (*El Dorado*, 1881) used instead. |
| "You can't cross the sea merely by standing and staring at the water." | Rabindranath Tagore | No source located; I did not run a full-text search of Tagore's works myself. |
| "It always seems impossible until it's done." | Nelson Mandela | No documented source located for Mandela; not checked against his speech archive by me. |
| "Faith is taking the first step even when you don't see the whole staircase." | Martin Luther King Jr. | Only late, second-hand attribution (attributed via Marian Wright Edelman). Not used. |
| "What you seek is seeking you." | Rumi | Generally regarded as a modern paraphrase; I did not check Nicholson/Arberry myself. |
| "Nothing in life is to be feared, it is only to be understood." | Marie Curie | Widely attributed; I could not find it in her own writings (*Pierre Curie*, 1923, searched). Possibly from a later biography — unverified, not used. |
| "Life is not easy for any of us. But what of that? We must have perseverance…" | Marie Curie | Appears in Eve Curie's biography (1937), not in Marie Curie's own text; could not see the page. Not used. |
| "Ancora imparo" ("I am still learning") | Michelangelo | No contemporary source; later legend. |
| "I write a little every day, without hope and without despair." | Karen Blixen | Commonly attributed; I could not locate the interview/source. Not used. |
| "Those who do not move, do not notice their chains." | Rosa Luxemburg | No source in her writings found. |
| "Never give up, for that is just the place and time that the tide will turn." | Harriet Beecher Stowe | Not traced to a Stowe text. |
| "Iron rusts from disuse; stagnant water loses its purity…" | Leonardo da Vinci | Loose, expanded rendering. The genuine Richter translation ("Just as iron rusts unless it is used…") is used instead as q013. |
| "I have been impressed with the urgency of doing. Knowing is not enough; we must apply." | Leonardo da Vinci | The "knowing is not enough" part is Goethe (*Maxims and Reflections*), used as q036. |
| "Obstacles are those frightful things you see when you take your eyes off your goal." | Henry Ford / Hannah More | No early source for either. |
| "He who has a why to live can bear almost any how." | Nietzsche | Genuine (Twilight of the Idols, Maxims §12) but this is Viktor Frankl's later paraphrase; Ludovici's 1911 published translation used instead (q026). |
| "The two most powerful warriors are patience and time." | Tolstoy | Genuine idea but this wording is not the Maude translation; Maude's wording used instead (q025). |
| "I am the wisest man alive, for I know one thing, and that is that I know nothing." | Socrates | Not a quotation from Plato's *Apology*; paraphrase. Off-theme anyway. |
| "The journey of a thousand miles begins with one step." | Laozi | Popular paraphrase; Legge's published wording ("thousand li") used instead (q006). |
| "Great things are not done by impulse, but by a series of small things brought together." | Van Gogh | Older (1958) translation of letter 274; the current Van Gogh Museum translation is used instead (q077) because it is the edition I could read with the Dutch alongside. Either is acceptable if the verifier prefers the older wording with its own citation. |
| "I know I have the body of a weak and feeble woman; but I have the heart and stomach of a king…" | Elizabeth I | Text survives only in a 1623/1654 letter by Leonel Sharp; authenticity of the wording is debated. Left out to keep the set clean. |
| "How wonderful it is that nobody need wait a single moment before starting to improve the world." | Anne Frank | Genuine (26 March 1944, "Give!"), but I could not confirm the published English translation's exact wording or a free-licence image. Not used. |
